What is actually running here. This page is the verdict engine, not a picture of it. Every number below — CIELAB conversion, CIEDE2000 distance, Individual Typology Angle, season selection, scoring — is computed in your browser from the swatches you pick. It is a direct port of core/colour.py, core/palette.py and core/verdict.py, which is why the engine is written as plain dependency-free arithmetic. Open devtools: the network tab stays empty.

How the garment is drawn. No image model. The motif is tiled into a clipped kurta silhouette and a shading layer is multiplied over it, which preserves the print exactly — a generative model redraws a print, and redrawn florals drift in scale and colour, which would quietly invalidate the numbers above. It costs nothing, needs no key, and renders in milliseconds. Crucially the motif is drawn at true relative size: change your height and watch the block print resize against the body, because that is what the print-scale verdict is actually talking about.

What is illustrative. The four fabrics use one real Khaadi SKU reference and three plausible ones. Their dominant colours are hand-specified stand-ins, and the motifs are drawn procedurally from those colours rather than being the real prints. The extraction pipeline that will measure both from product photography is built next, so nothing here has yet been measured off an actual garment. Swapping the procedural motif for a photographed template and a real extracted tile is what turns this into a render on a body double.

Why it stays free. Fabric analysis depends only on the SKU, never on who is looking, so it is computed once per catalogue and cached. A thousand shoppers browsing the same 2,000 fabrics cost exactly what one shopper costs.
